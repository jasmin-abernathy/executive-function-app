# Transition friction and problematic screen use — 2026-09-02

## Source type

Public literature synthesis and peer-reviewed studies.

## Observation

A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis of 15 studies (35,223 participants) found a moderate positive association between ADHD symptomatology and problematic social-media use (**r = 0.361**, 95% CI [0.297, 0.421]).

A 2026 community study of 111 emerging adults found direct associations between ADHD symptoms and problematic social-media use / gaming, but its main analyses did **not** find significant mediation through inhibitory control, reward sensitivity or temporal-processing deficits.

A public TDAH Focus synthesis proposes that the phone may become the easiest available action during difficult transitions such as getting up, starting a task or going to sleep. The article explicitly identifies this transition-specific explanation as plausible but **not yet tested in adults with ADHD**.

## Confidence / limitations

Confidence is moderate for the existence of an association between ADHD symptomatology and problematic social-media use.

Confidence is low for:

- the direction of causality;
- any specific dopamine-based explanation;
- the proposed cognitive mediators;
- the claim that transitions are the primary moment at which problematic use occurs;
- extrapolating general-population screen-reduction studies to adults with diagnosed ADHD.

Much of the available evidence is cross-sectional and/or self-reported. ADHD symptom scores are not equivalent to a clinical diagnosis, and an association does not demonstrate that screen use causes or worsens ADHD.

## Product hypothesis

When useful context already exists, restoring the user's saved intention or next physical step at a transition point may reduce the effort needed to resume.

The product opportunity is to protect intention, not to police screen time. This hypothesis supports testing a direct resume path, but it does not justify an app blocker, a “dopamine detox” feature, guilt-based limits or passive monitoring of other applications.

## Smallest test

Compare two prototype return states after a realistic or simulated interruption:

1. the standard Today / dashboard view;
2. a direct resume card showing the preserved task context and next physical step.

With explicit participant consent, observe:

- time to the first useful action;
- whether the intended action is remembered;
- perceived effort and pressure;
- whether the interface creates unwanted additional screen time.

The test must not require passive application-usage tracking, accessibility-service monitoring or app-blocking permissions.

## Decision

**Test.**

This insight does not change the P1 scope. It sharpens the existing interruption / resume flow and adds a validation question before any broader anti-distraction feature is considered.
